# First real pilot story

The first pilot should prove depth, trust and usefulness — not catalogue size.

## Candidate selection

Choose one living practice where:
- a practitioner or community is willing to participate;
- direct communication is possible;
- the practice has meaningful person-to-person transmission;
- local-language documentation adds value;
- there is enough evidence to responsibly describe context;
- the story does not require publishing protected knowledge.

Do not choose a person merely because the story sounds dramatic.

## Field interview sequence

1. **Meet the person** — how they describe themselves.
2. **Meet the place** — workshop/home/landscape and its role.
3. **Meet the practice** — what they do, in their words.
4. **Meet the lineage** — who taught them and how.
5. **Meet the judgement** — what cannot be learned from instructions alone.
6. **Meet the future** — who is learning, what makes transmission difficult.
7. **Ask the key prompt** — “What is one thing someone should know after you?”
8. **Set boundaries** — what should not be recorded or shared.
9. **Review** — practitioner sees/hears the intended story before publication.

## Capture

Prefer natural conversation over scripted performance. Record original-language audio cleanly, plus environmental sound. Photograph hands, tools, materials, place and process without turning the person into an exotic object.

## Minimum publishable package

- practitioner-approved profile;
- original-language audio/video excerpt;
- reviewed transcript and translation;
- practice context;
- consented lineage relationship(s);
- one tacit-knowledge memory;
- evidence-backed preservation assessment or “insufficient evidence”;
- source/evidence notes;
- access permissions;
- last reviewed date.

## Success criteria

A visitor should leave knowing **a person**, not merely remembering the name of a craft.
